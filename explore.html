Explore Cameroon

Discover 11 remarkable tourist sites, 5 vibrant cultural traditions, and 9 signature meals.

Tourist Sites Across Cameroon

Mountains, Lakes & Wildlife Reserves

Mount Fako

Cameroon’s highest peak and an active volcano rising above the Atlantic coast, renowned for dramatic trails and highland views.

Mount Rhumsiki

Famous for dramatic volcanic plugs, rugged rock spires, and sweeping northern landscapes that draw hikers and photographers.

Twin Lakes of Manenguba

Two scenic crater lakes—traditionally known as male and female—nestled in lush highland terrain.

Mbati Lake

A peaceful natural lake setting offering quiet scenery, fresh air, and connection with surrounding local communities.

Waza National Park

A premier northern wildlife reserve protecting savanna habitats, elephants, giraffes, birds, and seasonal floodplains.

Dja Reserve

A dense tropical rainforest reserve celebrated for rich biodiversity, forest wildlife, and traditional forest stewardship.

Coast, Waterfalls & Monuments

Black Kribi Beach

Distinctive dark coastal sands along the Atlantic shore, ideal for relaxing by the waves and enjoying coastal breezes.

Chute de Lobé

An iconic waterfall site near Kribi where cascading waters plunge directly into the Atlantic Ocean.

Monument de la Nouvelle Liberté

A towering urban landmark in Douala standing as a bold symbol of resilience, creativity, and civic identity.

Monument de la Réunification

A major historical monument in Yaoundé commemorating national unity and milestones in Cameroon’s history.

Mfoumban Palace

A historic royal palace showcasing centuries of Bamoun heritage, regal architecture, and traditional craftsmanship.

Traditions & Cultural Communities

Cameroon’s cultural richness comes alive through distinct regional traditions, attire, music, and ways of life.

Grassfield People

Known for structured chiefdoms, ceremonial regalia, royal dances, and vibrant highland festivals.

Islamic Communities

Marked by rich northern hospitality, ceremonial horsemanship, colorful robes, and enduring religious and trade traditions.

Center Beti-Fang People

Celebrated for lively rhythms, storytelling, communal feasts, and expressive traditional performances.

Sawa People

Coastal and riverine communities deeply tied to the water, maritime traditions, and festive gatherings.

Pygmy Communities

Forest peoples with deep ecological knowledge of medicinal plants, harmony with the woodland, and polyphonic music.

Cameroonian Meals (Hover or Focus to Flip in 3D)

Ndolé au Fritures

Ndolé au Fritures

Rich leafy greens and groundnut stew paired with crispy fried sides for an unmistakable Cameroonian classic.

Mbongo Tchobi

Mbongo Tchobi

A bold, aromatic dark spice stew traditionally prepared with roasted spices and fish or meat.

Okok Sucré

Okok Sucré

Finely shredded forest leaves cooked with groundnuts and a touch of sweetness—a favorite across central and forest tables.

Sanga

Sanga

Fresh maize, cassava leaves, and palm nut juice simmered together into a comforting traditional delicacy.

Beignet-Haricot-Bouilli

Beignet-Haricot-Bouilli

Crispy golden puffs (beignets) served with spiced beans (haricot) and hot pap/porridge (bouilli).

Koki & Plantain

Koki & Plantain

Steamed cowpea pudding seasoned with palm oil and spices, enjoyed alongside ripe or boiled plantains.

Poisson Braisé au Plantain

Poisson Braisé au Plantain

Charcoal-grilled fish marinated in local herbs and spices, served with plantains and spicy pepper sauce.

Prune & Maïs Braisé

Prune & Maïs Braisé

A beloved seasonal pairing of roasted corn and rich African plums (safou) bursting with smoky flavor.

Egusi Soup

Egusi Soup

Hearty melon-seed soup enriched with greens, spices, and savory cuts of meat or fish.

Activities & Travel Resources

Mountain & Crater Lake Hikes

Explore volcanic slopes and crater views at Mount Fako, Mount Rhumsiki, and the Twin Lakes of Manenguba.

Coastal & Waterfall Excursions

Walk along Black Kribi Beach and take a boat excursion near Chute de Lobé.

Cultural & Wildlife Discovery

Visit Mfoumban Palace, city monuments, and protected reserves like Waza Park and Dja Reserve.
